Overview

Why AI for 6G security

As 6G networks move toward software-defined, densely connected, and increasingly autonomous architectures, they inherit a much larger attack surface than earlier generations — spanning the radio access network, network slices, and the AI models now embedded inside the network stack itself. Securing this environment calls for AI-native defenses, not just AI-aware ones.

AI for Secure 6G: Foundations of FL, XAI, and LLMs brings together researchers, practitioners, and students to explore how techniques such as federated learning, explainable AI, and large language models can be applied to detect threats, harden network intelligence, and build trust into 6G systems — while also examining the new vulnerabilities that AI itself introduces into the network.

About Course Coordinators

Dr. Neetesh Kumar

Dr. Neetesh Kumar is an Associate Professor in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at IIT Roorkee. He earned his M.Tech and PhD from Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi. His research focuses on Artificial Intelligence, Cloud Computing, Transport Cyber-Physical Systems, and Vehicular Networking, with work published in journals like IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems. He is Lead PI on several government-funded research projects and has received a Special Mention Award from Xerox Research Centre, Bangalore, along with multiple best paper awards.

Dr. Abhay Kumar Sah

Dr. Abhay Kumar Sah is an Associate Professor in the Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering at IIT Roorkee. He earned his PhD in Wireless Communication from IIT Kanpur, and previously worked as a Technical Lead at Samsung R&D and Lead Architect at Radisys India. His research covers 5G/6G systems, cell-free massive MIMO, and hybrid RF/VLC systems, with publications in IEEE Transactions on Communications and top conferences like GLOBECOM and ICC, along with several granted patents.

About SPARC

Scheme for Promotion of Academic and Research Collaboration

SPARC is an initiative of the Ministry of Education, Government of India, that facilitates academic and research collaboration between Indian institutions and top-ranked institutes abroad. It supports joint research, student and faculty exchange, and knowledge-sharing activities — including workshops like this one — designed to strengthen India's research ecosystem through international partnership.

About the Institute

Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee

The Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee has the distinction of being the first engineering college in the Indian subcontinent. Founded as Roorkee College in 1847, it was later renamed Thomason College of Civil Engineering, and in 2001 became the seventh IIT in India. Declared an Institute of National Importance, IIT Roorkee is dedicated to excellence in engineering, science, and technology education and research.

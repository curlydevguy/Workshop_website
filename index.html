Overview

Why AI for 6G security

As 6G networks move toward software-defined, densely connected, and increasingly autonomous architectures, they inherit a much larger attack surface than earlier generations — spanning the radio access network, network slices, and the AI models now embedded inside the network stack itself. Securing this environment calls for AI-native defenses, not just AI-aware ones.

AI for 6G Security brings together researchers, practitioners, and students to explore how techniques such as federated learning, explainable AI, and large language models can be applied to detect threats, harden network intelligence, and build trust into 6G systems — while also examining the new vulnerabilities that AI itself introduces into the network.

Keynote Speakers

International Keynote Speakers

Prof. Dushantha Nalin K Jayakody

Professor, Lusófona University, Portugal

Prof. Jayakody is a Professor at Lusófona University, Portugal, and an IEEE Senior Member whose research centers on physical-layer security, coding theory, and wireless communications for 5G/6G networks. His work spans non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA), visible light communication, and energy-harvesting relay networks, and he regularly serves as an editor and reviewer for leading IEEE journals — bringing a strong systems-and-standards perspective to how AI-driven security can be engineered directly into next-generation wireless infrastructure.

Official webpage →

Prof. Madhusanka Liyanage

Professor & Ad Astra Fellow, School of Computer Science, University College Dublin, Ireland

Prof. Liyanage is a Professor and Ad Astra Fellow at the School of Computer Science, University College Dublin, where his research centers on the security and softwarization of 5G/6G mobile networks. He works extensively on network slicing security, O-RAN, and blockchain-based trust models for telecom infrastructure, and is an active contributor to international standardization efforts and 6G research initiatives — making him one of the sharper voices on what it actually takes to secure next-generation network architectures at scale.

About SPARC

Scheme for Promotion of Academic and Research Collaboration

SPARC is an initiative of the Ministry of Education, Government of India, that facilitates academic and research collaboration between Indian institutions and top-ranked institutes abroad. It supports joint research, student and faculty exchange, and knowledge-sharing activities — including workshops like this one — designed to strengthen India's research ecosystem through international partnership.

About the Institute

Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee

The Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee has the distinction of being the first engineering college in the Indian subcontinent. Founded as Roorkee College in 1847, it was later renamed Thomason College of Civil Engineering, and in 2001 became the seventh IIT in India. Declared an Institute of National Importance, IIT Roorkee is dedicated to excellence in engineering, science, and technology education and research.
